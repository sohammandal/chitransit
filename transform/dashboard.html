A trip is a silent ghost only after the innocent explanations are ruled out: no bus reported it (matched on pattern and start, or route and start for detours, including a bus on its pattern under another trip key); the feed was swept for ≥ 90% of it; no detour ran on the route within the hour; its block's bus did not vanish and reappear at the trip's far end (a dead tracker); CTA's predictions at its own stops were sampled at least twice before it was due (at stops known to show cancellations; a canceled trip is missing from about a quarter of looks) and never flagged it, nor a flag CTA later cleared. Strict counts only ghosts with physical evidence the bus didn't run it: it was seen doing something else during the trip, or reappeared where the trip started. Lenient counts all. Missing: the same, but its stops weren't sampled, so we can't say whether CTA announced it. Delivered = ran ÷ (ran + announced + silent + missing). Bunched: headway under 25% of scheduled; gaps: over 2×; as a share of stop passes, terminals excluded.

Watched: the feed was swept for ≥ 90% of the trip's minutes; the rest are unknown, never ghosts. Detour window: unknown because the route ran patterns its schedule lacks within the hour. Announcement known: of the trips no bus ran, the share whose stops CTA's predictions were sampled at often enough to say whether it was announced (silent ÷ (silent + missing)). Likely ran: unseen or seen once, counted as ran.

Method and assumptions

RuleAssumesEvidenceHow it could be wrong
A bus runs the scheduled trip with the same route, pattern and start (GTFS start 0–120 s after the bus's scheduled start); else same route and start CTA's scheduled start on the bus names the GTFS trip 271 of 271 observed trips matched exactly one scheduled trip in a 1-hour all-route check; 60 s or 180 s tolerance moves silent by ≤ 5 A trip run far off its start looks missing: detour windows and a nightly check for unmatched buses near silent trips guard it
Judge a trip only if the feed was swept for ≥ 90% of its minutes A bus absent from a complete sweep wasn't reporting Sweeps every 30 s (see Watched above); 80% or 95% moves silent by ≤ 2 Feed-wide outages: those trips are unknown, never ghosts
Seen once, or its block's bus vanished and reappeared at the trip's far end, or was on its pattern under another key: likely ran Buses drop off the tracker on hardware failures while still running CTA documents tracker dropouts; 250 m or 1,000 m reappearance moves counts by ≤ 2 A bus that drove there empty is counted as having run it: ghosts are undercounted, not overcounted
Announced = CTA flagged it canceled (dyn = 1) at a stop the trip serves, and never cleared the flag The prediction flag is CTA's public cancellation notice Predictions sampled every 5 min at ~730 first and middle stops; no flag has been cleared later so far CTA hides some cancel types (dyn = 12) and some stops don't show cancellations
Silent ghost = no bus, not announced, and its own stops were sampled ≥ 2 times in the 30 min before it was due, at stops known to show cancellations; else missing A canceled trip appears as canceled in most looks at its stops A canceled trip showed in 73% of looks; 2 of 53 canceled trips with ≥ 4 looks were missed on every look A few percent of announced cancellations may be counted as silent: the lenient count is an upper bound
Strict = the block's bus was seen doing something else during the trip, or reappeared where it started The bus that ran the block's trip before (or after) would have run this one Physical: positions, not the absence of them A substitute bus with a dead tracker could have run it; the nightly check for unmatched buses near silent trips looks for that
Detours: a route running patterns its schedule lacks makes its trips within an hour unknown; CTA Customer Alerts are kept as context CTA gives detours their own patterns Long-running reroutes are already in the schedule; routes under reroute alerts had no excess silent trips (Sep 26) A reroute on regular patterns can make buses vanish; watched nightly
